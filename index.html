Elo model, by season home-favorite baseline

Ratings are computed client-side from every NFL regular-season and playoff result since 1999 (via the public nflverse game log, including Pro-Football-Reference's closing spread and total lines), embedded in this page as a fixed snapshot — reopen the source chat to refresh it with newer results. Home-field advantage (+48 Elo), a margin-of-victory multiplier, and 1/3 season-to-season regression to the mean all follow FiveThirtyEight's published NFL Elo methodology. Spread calls model final margin as normally distributed around the Elo-implied line. Total-points calls run a separate points-for/points-against rating per team and model the combined score the same way, recalibrated against a small but persistent bias (the raw model ran about six tenths of a point hot on totals) and slowed down to a 5%-per-game learning rate, which backtests cleaner than the faster rate this page originally shipped with.

On why the spread and total picks won't hit the high-50s: backtesting every closing line since 1999 shows this model's own edge is real but thin — about 51% straight-up against the spread and roughly a coin flip on totals, both essentially in line with how efficient the closing NFL market is by kickoff. The published benchmark for a considerably more sophisticated public model (nfelo, which layers in QB-adjusted ratings) is 55.24% against closing lines — and that's with real information (starter changes, injuries) this page doesn't have. The one place a genuine edge shows up here: games where this model and the closing line disagree by a wide margin. Restricting the "most confident" picks to only those disagreements (3+ points for spreads, 1+ for totals) lifts the backtested walk-forward record to roughly 53% on spreads and 52% on totals — and the single biggest disagreement of the week, alone, has hit about 56% historically. Diluting that down to three picks a week costs some of that edge, which is why the 2nd and 3rd "confident" picks each week are markedly weaker than the 1st. Treat all of it as a well-tested statistical baseline, not betting advice.
